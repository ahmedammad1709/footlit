# Footlit marketing foundation

Written 2026-10-16, after 18 organic installs. Evidence from public marketing guides is directional (mostly vendor blogs, figures unverified); the structure below leans on points that several sources agree on and on how developer audiences behave.

## 1. Positioning (say this the same way everywhere)

- **What it is:** a free browser extension that gives every GitHub moment its own show.
- **Who it is for:** developers who live on GitHub (first), then streamers, bootcamp students, small teams, indie hackers.
- **The one-line promise:** "Merge a PR and get a standing ovation." (Alternative: "GitHub, with stage lighting.")
- **Why believe it (proof points):** free, one click, six hand-built themes, eleven events, github.com only, nothing leaves your browser, no account.
- **What it is not:** not an app, not mobile, desktop browsers only. Never imply otherwise (honesty protects the "private and clean" reputation).

## 2. The video: what to change

The 56 s film is well made, but as marketing its weak spot is the first 17 seconds: the viewer sees a lot of beautiful themes before they know what the thing is or why they should care. Guides agree on three points: stop the scroll in the first 1 to 3 seconds, make the product or its result visible in the very first frame, and show the brand name early but briefly.

**Recommended structure for the next cut (about 35 to 40 s, with a 15 to 20 s version for Reels):**

| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2.5 s | The hook: a developer clicks "Merge pull request". Nothing happens. Caption: "You just shipped. Nothing happened." | Problem first, instantly recognisable to the exact audience |
| 2.5 to 4.5 s | The payoff: BRAVO sign, confetti, applause. Caption: "Footlit" | Result visible early, brand introduced in one second |
| 4.5 to 6 s | "A free GitHub extension." with the logo | Category clear |
| 6 to 22 s | Three real moments: create a repo, break the build (alarm), merge (applause) | Proof: it works on real GitHub flows |
| 22 to 30 s | Fast themes montage, one caption: "Six themes." | The wow, now with context |
| 30 to 36 s | Trust line: "Free. Private. Nothing leaves your browser." then store link / URL | Remove the last objection, one call to action |

Rules for every cut: burned-in captions (a large share of viewers watch muted), one idea per shot, no shot longer than the viewer needs, the call to action stated once, hook sentence identical across platforms.

**Test before you commit:** watch only the first 1.5 s on mute. Can a stranger say what it is?

## 3. Platform plan

| Platform | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts / TikTok | 15 to 35 s vertical, captions burned in | The hook decides everything. Post the same video on all three. |
| LinkedIn | Native upload (not a link), 30 to 60 s, captions, a founder note | Tell the "why I built it" story; ask a question to start discussion |
| X / Twitter | The 12 to 20 s clip plus a one-line hook | Reply to your own post with the store link |
| Show HN | "Show HN: Footlit, a browser extension that celebrates your GitHub merges" | Honest, short first comment, live link that holds up, expect blunt feedback |
| Reddit | r/webdev, r/github, r/SideProject, r/chrome_extensions | Read each subreddit's self-promotion rules first. Share the clip and ask for feedback; do not spam |
| Product Hunt / DevHunt | A polished listing with the demo video | Product Hunt suits polish; DevHunt suits developer tools |
| Directories | AlternativeTo, daily.dev, dev.to / Hashnode write-up | Long-tail search over time |

## 4. Launch week (suggested)

- **Day 0:** LinkedIn founder post + the new video; Instagram Reel + story; link in every bio.
- **Day 1:** X thread (problem, demo, three themes, link); first Reddit post (one subreddit only).
- **Day 2:** Show HN (weekday morning US time works best for developer audiences).
- **Day 3 to 5:** Product Hunt / DevHunt; reply to every comment and review within a day.
- **Week 2:** a series: one 10 s clip per theme or per event ("PR merged on Terminal", "build failed on Mission Control").

## 5. Content engine (steady posting)

Short, repeatable formats: "reaction" clips per theme, "watch me ruin the build" (failed-checks alarm), "same merge, six themes", a release/changelog clip for each version, a behind-the-scenes "how the animation engine works" for developers.

## 6. Reviews and social proof

- Ask real users for honest reviews: launch posts, replies, the website footer. Never offer anything in exchange and never post fake reviews (store policy).
- Reply to every review; for bugs say which version fixed it.
- Put one real, permissioned user quote on the site once you have it.

## 7. What to measure

- Video: 3 s hold, average watch time, completion rate, shares and saves (shares signal reach most strongly).
- Funnel: site visits, clicks on "Add to Chrome" (cookie-free analytics), store installs, store reviews and rating.
- Weekly: write the numbers in one spreadsheet (installs from each store, video completion, comments). Change one thing at a time.

## 8. Next experiments (in order)

1. Re-cut the hero film with the hook-first structure above, plus a 20 s vertical version.
2. Post one Reel per theme for two weeks and see which theme gets the most completion and shares.
3. Show HN and Reddit with the honest demo clip.
4. Consider a small in-product "share this moment" feature later (a screenshot of your BRAVO with the repo name), because shared proof is the best marketing a delight product can have.
